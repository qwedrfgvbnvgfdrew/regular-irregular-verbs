Every verb has three forms.

Verbs change when we talk about the past. Regular verbs follow a rule. Irregular verbs break it, so you have to learn them. Type any verb below to see its forms.

Base form

The dictionary form: walk, go. Use it with I, you, we, they.

Past simple

A finished action: “Yesterday I walked home.”

Past participle

Used with have, has, had: “I have walked home.”

Regular verbs: add -ed

Past simple and past participle are the same. Choose the right spelling rule:

Most verbs: add -ed

walk → walked → walked

play → played → played

Ends in -e: add -d

like → liked → liked

dance → danced → danced

Consonant + y: change y to -ied

study → studied → studied

cry → cried → cried

But vowel + y just adds -ed: play → played

Short verb ending consonant-vowel-consonant: double the last letter

stop → stopped → stopped

plan → planned → planned

Not for w, x, y: fix → fixed

How to say -ed

/t/

After sounds like p, k, f, s, sh, ch.

walked, helped, washed, watched

/d/

After most other sounds.

played, cleaned, loved, opened

/ɪd/ (an extra syllable)

After t or d.

wanted, needed, started, decided
